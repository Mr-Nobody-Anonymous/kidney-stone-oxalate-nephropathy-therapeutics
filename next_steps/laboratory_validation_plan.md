# Laboratory validation plan — NOT performed

For a qualified polymer chemistry / pharmaceutical research laboratory. No human ingestion, self-experimentation, animal dosing, or clinical treatment is authorized by this document. A material meeting this brief has not been manufactured or shown safe. No synthesis procedure is supplied.

## 1. Concrete screening-material specification
**Research family:** covalently crosslinked poly(allylamine)-based hydrogel particles, with a fraction of pendant amines converted to guanidinium-containing groups and, in coated variants, a covalently attached hydrated PEG-rich surface layer. This is a proposed materials family, not a validated formulation or novel invention.

**Lead screening label:** PAA-G25-PEG. The label means a target of 25 mol% of precursor pendant amine sites converted to guanidinium-containing groups, with a PEG-rich exterior. The 25% value is a screening level, not an optimized result. PEG chain length, graft density, crosslink chemistry/density, size distribution, counterion, and manufacturing route remain unresolved and must be selected and documented by the materials team. Consequently, this is NOT yet a fully reproducible chemical identity. Do not assign the aspirational simulation properties to this material.

**Matched series:** G0, G10, and G25 variants (0%, 10%, and 25% target substitution), each with and without the surface layer. Match measured dry accessible site content and particle characteristics where possible. Evaluate at least three independent manufactured batches per selected variant, with technical replicates; batch—not each instrument injection—is the independent experimental unit.

The initial proposal is deliberately conditional. Abandon the family if membrane interactions, instability, or nonspecific binding make it unsuitable. Do not assume large particle size or crosslinking proves nonabsorption.

**Identity record required before testing:** lot ID; precursor molecular-weight distribution; measured substitution; crosslink identity/content; counterion; residual reactive reagents/solvents; soluble fraction; surface chemistry; dry and hydrated particle-size distributions; accessible site concentration; water uptake; stability through pH cycling; and storage conditions. Use appropriate complementary analytical methods selected by the laboratory (e.g., spectroscopy, elemental analysis, chromatography for leachables, microscopy/particle sizing). Exact numerical release specifications require toxicological and manufacturing justification.

## 2. Comparator and control panel
- No binder, full digestive mixture.
- Unfunctionalized matched network, to separate backbone and site effects.
- Noncoated and coated matched variants, to measure diffusion versus fouling trade-offs.
- Calcium-based oxalate binding comparator under matched food-like conditions; use equal material mass and separately matched measured capacity comparisons.
- Sevelamer carbonate as a research comparator, where appropriately procured and handled—not a recommendation to use it for this condition.
- Analytical blanks, spike-recovery controls, material-only blanks, and an oxalate assay interference control.

Keep precipitation and adsorption distinguishable: disappearance of soluble oxalate alone does not prove selective, retained polymer binding. Calcium can precipitate oxalate. Quantify relevant solid and liquid pools and close the mass balance.

## 3. Binding / realistic mixture test matrix
Test binding isotherms and time courses across sequential gastric, small-intestinal, and colonic-like conditions. The laboratory should use a recognized simulated digestion framework, justify extensions for colon conditions, and document its deviations. Simulated digestion remains an approximation of humans.

Vary oxalate loading, pH, ionic strength/chloride, phosphate, citrate, calcium/magnesium, bile salts, protein, lipid, and standardized meal matrix. Include low/high challenge conditions and a release phase. Avoid choosing only conditions that favor the candidate. The numerical ranges in the computational script are exploratory assumptions—not a validated digestion recipe.

Measure free oxalate and total recoverable oxalate with a validated analytical method such as ion chromatography or a validated enzyme-based method with matrix interference controls. Measure bound material using validated extraction or an independently validated mass-balance approach. Record analytical detection limits, recovery, uncertainty, and assay interference.

**Outputs:** accessible capacity (mmol/g dry material), effective mixture-dependent affinity/isotherm, kinetic time course, uncertainty, and selectivity relative to individual competing solutes. Do not fit a single-site model if residuals indicate multiple sites or precipitation.

## 4. Downstream retention / release
Load material in the upstream mixture, quantify loaded oxalate, and then transfer it through downstream pH/ionic/meal conditions. Measure released oxalate at multiple times and remaining retained oxalate at the end. Correct for sample removal, dilution, transfer losses, and assay recovery. Include unloaded particles and no-particle controls.

Report both:
- final retained fraction of initial accessible oxalate;
- final retained fraction of oxalate actually loaded onto the material.

A 70% initial capture result cannot substitute for a 70% end-of-transit retention result. Site deactivation, fragmentation, displacement, and slow downstream release can all undermine benefit.

## 5. Safety and off-target panel — cannot be inferred from binding simulations
Use a toxicologist-approved, fit-for-purpose program. Initial endpoints should include epithelial cell viability, barrier integrity/permeability, membrane damage, inflammatory response, and the effects of material extracts/leachables. Include positive, negative, and assay-interference controls and assess interference from particles.

Measure swelling and fragmentation through the digestion sequence, particle aggregation, soluble fractions, and extractables/leachables. Evaluate possible uptake of fragments; a nominally nonabsorbed label must be demonstrated, not presumed. Longer-term microbiome and gastrointestinal tolerability questions remain separate.

Measure binding of phosphate, citrate, bile acids, calcium, magnesium, iron, representative nutrients, and clinically relevant coadministered medicines selected with a pharmacist. Selective adsorption cannot be assumed from charge or particle size. Simulated drug binding is not a substitute for interaction testing.

Passing cell assays does not establish human safety. Animal or human research requires additional justification, approvals, validated materials, and oversight.

## 6. Bias control and analysis
- Register the primary endpoint before data collection: final retained oxalate as a fraction of initial accessible oxalate in a prespecified mixture panel.
- Freeze material identities, mixtures, comparator conditions, exclusions, and analysis before unblinding.
- Randomize sample order and blind analytical operators to material identity.
- Use independent batches; report all failed batches and excluded runs with reasons.
- Report individual batch results and appropriate uncertainty; do not treat technical replicates as independent batches.
- Use pilot variance to calculate confirmatory batch numbers and power. Three batches are an exploratory starting point, not a powered efficacy study.
- Preserve raw chromatograms/readouts and fit diagnostics. Do not enter simulated numbers in experimental result fields.

## 7. Proposed engineering gates—not validated clinical criteria
1. Analytical method and recovery are adequate for the specified decision, with prospectively justified bounds.
2. Identity and batch reproducibility are established; impurities/leachables reviewed by a toxicologist.
3. Exploratory goal: at least 70% final retained oxalate across the predefined realistic-mixture panel. For a confirmatory claim, require a prespecified statistical analysis, appropriate sample size, and a justified lower-confidence-bound criterion.
4. Demonstrate advantage over controls in the same experiment at a practical material burden; report capacity-normalized and mass-normalized comparisons.
5. No unresolved safety signal, excessive swelling, or unacceptable off-target binding. Quantitative safety and interaction limits must be justified by relevant exposure and toxicology—not invented here.
6. Refit and lock a model using measured inputs, then test its predictions in independent mixtures/batches. Failing any essential gate means revise or stop; reaching 70% capture does not override safety failures.

## 8. Deliverable from the laboratory
Completed measurement template, raw data, material identity dossier, assay validation, deviations, blinded analysis report, and a clear go/revise/stop recommendation. The current computational project has none of those experimental results.
