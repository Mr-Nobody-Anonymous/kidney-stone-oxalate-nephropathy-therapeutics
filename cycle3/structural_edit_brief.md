# Structural edit brief: a published oxalate receptor, not a new validated drug

## Evidence-backed starting scaffold
TPPC³⁺·3Cl⁻, the trimethyl pinwheel-shaped pyridinium bicyclic amide cage described by Xu et al. (Chemical Science, 2024; DOI 10.1039/D4SC05236F).

The authors describe a preorganized cage with six inward-facing amide NH donors and three pyridinium panels. They report strong oxalate binding in water. This receptor is already published; we did not discover it. This document is not a synthesis procedure or an assertion of patentability.

## Proposed edit E1: attach without opening the cage
**Working research label:** PEG-network–link6–TPPC.

At ONE pyridinium nitrogen, investigate replacing the parent N-methyl substituent with a tether-bearing N-alkyl substituent. A schematic terminal connection is:

Parent module: `cage–N⁺–CH3`

Proposed module: `cage–N⁺–CH2CH2CH2–O–(CH2CH2O)6–CH2–[covalent network attachment]`

The expression is a connectivity sketch for a proposed substituent—not a complete SMILES, molecular identity, or manufacturing specification. The cage topology must remain that of the published scaffold. The other two pyridinium N-methyl groups and all six amide NH donors are intended to remain unchanged. The network attachment chemistry and full crosslinked-network composition remain to be selected and verified. “link6” denotes a screening choice, not a proven optimal linker.

**Hypothesis:** a sufficiently exposed, tethered cavity could retain useful binding while the support limits mobility of the intact receptor. Neither cavity accessibility nor nonabsorption follows automatically from this structure. Receptor orientation, attachment geometry, water structure, and conformational effects must be checked against the published structure and new structural characterization.

**Risks:** the larger N-substituent may change cage shape, hydration, apparent affinity, counterion occupancy, or aggregation. The support may block the cavity. Covalent attachment may fail, leach, or degrade. Large particles may shed smaller fragments.

## Edit E2: neutral support rather than a broadly cationic backbone
Investigate a crosslinked PEG-rich support with neutral bulk functional groups, carrying discrete cage sites rather than many exposed protonatable amines. This is a conceptual alternative to the original guanidinated poly(allylamine) family.

**Hypothesis:** fewer exposed nonspecific cationic backbone sites may reduce unwanted binding/contact. This is NOT a safety prediction. The cage itself remains tricationic, the material could still bind nutrients/medicines, and the support and its impurities need independent assessment.

**Cost:** the receptor and support add mass, potentially lowering mmol of accessible pockets per gram. High affinity alone does not solve material burden. No practical dose is established.

## Edit E3: access / shielding series
Compare an uncoated tethered receptor with low and higher surface shielding, and short/medium/long linkers. Prospectively measure accessible capacity, uptake time course, and downstream release for each. Do not assert that PEG selectively excludes small phosphate or sulfate ions: shielding may exclude proteins yet do little to distinguish small competing anions.

The simulated “10× lower competition” variant is a desired property, not a calculated consequence of PEG, linker length, or this structural edit. A material that actually achieved that property could require a different cavity architecture; its feasibility is unknown.

## Parts to preserve unless evidence justifies changes
- Convergent amide NH donors: do not cap/methylate them merely to alter charge; doing so removes potential hydrogen-bond donors.
- Cage geometry: do not infer that a flexible open-chain analog has the same binding properties.
- Pyridinium charge pattern: changing it may change affinity, solubility, interactions and toxicity together.

These are mechanistic hypotheses based on the published receptor architecture, not measured structure–activity relationships for our derivatives.

## What has NOT been accomplished
No edited molecule was synthesized, isolated, chemically validated, assigned a verified full structure, or subjected to molecular free-energy calculations. The sequential digestion simulations do not evaluate the atoms in this sketch. They evaluate conditional affinity/capacity/access parameters. No modified receptor has a demonstrated binding constant, selectivity, safety profile, or novelty claim.

## Next molecular-computation specification
Before claiming a molecular prediction, obtain/verify the published atomic structure; generate chemically valid, fully specified derivatives and protonation/counterion states; assess conformer/cavity geometry; and use a validated aqueous host–guest method with explicit water, ions, and competing anions. Calibrate against parent experimental data and report uncertainty. Simple vacuum electrostatic scores or generic protein docking would not establish intestinal binding or safety.
